The Seventh Lamp

The abandoned wada above the mango orchard
Original fiction, inspired by Konkan & Saurashtra village tales

On the laterite hill above the mango orchard stands a wada that has been empty since 1952, when a fever took six children of the house in one monsoon. Six brass lamps still hang on its verandah, one for each child, and on every new-moon midnight they light themselves. The old keeper, Ganpat, taught the village one rule: count the flames. If a seventh lamp glows, someone in the village will die within seven days. In October 1984, the schoolmaster counted seven. Before the week ended, his younger brother drowned in the flooded stepwell.

"Whisper: Count the flames."

The Voice Under the Peepal

The dry riverbed near Junagadh
Oral lore of the Saurashtra cart runners

Travellers who cross the dry bed after twilight are told never to answer if someone calls their name twice. In 1934, a postal runner named Nathu heard his mother's voice calling from beneath the twisted peepal roots. When he turned back with his lantern, he found only a pile of white cowrie shells arranged in the shape of a hand. Nathu never spoke another sentence of Gujarati again, whispering only in syllables of old Prakrit until the day he died.

"Whisper: A voice that sounds like someone you love."

The Salt Merchant's Dice

The coastal watchtower of Mandvi
Historical coastal mystery, 1944

The sea-facing timber doors of the merchant's room have been chained shut with iron padlocks since 1944. During low tide, fishermen hear ivory dice rattling across a teak gaming board on the upper floor. If you shine a flashlight through the keyhole, two copper cups of cold tea sit steaming on the table. The watchman recorded in his logbook that the game began eighty years ago, and neither player has stood up from the chairs since.

"Whisper: The tea stays hot, even after eighty years."
